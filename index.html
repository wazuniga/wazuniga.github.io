W. A. Zúñiga-Galindo

Debnath Endowed Professor in Mathematical Sciences
School of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences
The University of Texas Rio Grande Valley
Edinburg, Texas, United States

My research develops the mathematics of hierarchical structure — non-Archimedean (p-adic) analysis, pseudodifferential operators, and statistical field theory — and, over the past five years, has applied it to the mathematical foundations of deep learning. I have written three research monographs and more than eighty papers, and have supervised seventeen doctoral dissertations in Colombia, Mexico, and the United States.

Research areas

  • Mathematical foundations of deep learning A correspondence between deep Boltzmann machines and p-adic statistical field theories; universal approximation for non-Archimedean architectures; dynamic mean-field theory and criticality; thermodynamic limits and phase transitions in deep and recurrent networks.
  • Pseudodifferential equations and Markov processes Parabolic-type equations driven by non-local operators over ultrametric spaces, the Feller semigroups and jump processes they generate, and stochastic versions of the Cauchy problem.
  • Local zeta functions, oscillatory integrals, and string amplitudes Igusa's theory and Newton polyhedra, zeta functions for meromorphic functions, and the meromorphic continuation of Koba–Nielsen string amplitudes.
  • Quantum mechanics over a discrete space p-Adic Schrödinger and Dirac equations, continuous-time quantum walks on hierarchical networks, and questions in quantum foundations.
  • Models of complex hierarchical systems Reaction–diffusion dynamics and Turing patterns on networks, hierarchical Wilson–Cowan models of cortical activity, and non-Archimedean approaches to the quasispecies model.
